# Paint Pallet

## Goal

Build an offline Windows app that turns reference images and a 3D miniature into a printable painting and installation guide. The first release supports both single-piece models and multipart STL kits. Support up to 2 GiB of combined full-resolution binary STL sources per project on a Windows 11 x64 PC with 32 GB RAM and SSD storage. The supplied single-piece benchmark is 418 MiB with 8,766,580 triangles. The segmented kit has 33 main STLs and three alternative base pieces, about 1.15 GiB altogether. Keep those assets local and use generated fixtures for automated tests.

## Assemble the kit

- Start with a fixed anchor part and set its upright direction and front. Import additional STLs one at a time or stage several at once. Preserve their exported coordinates under the same display transform so kits exported in one coordinate system can open aligned. Name and inspect parts independently. For an already aligned part, select its mating parent and mark the joint, then confirm the current pose to add it to the installation guide.
- For a part that needs alignment, move it near the intended joint and use **Snap nearby**. Compare nearby original mesh surfaces to refine rotation and translation, optionally restricting the search to a selected parent. Show the proposed joint and fit in a ghost preview. Keep the current pose if the fit is weak, penetrating, ambiguous, or too far away. Confirm to record the same joint as in the other attachment methods.
- Pick the peg tip and two adjacent sides, then the socket floor and its corresponding walls in the same order. Highlight each connected planar face, including when it spans several STL triangles, and make the selected face clear when hovering near an edge. Allow stepping back through the picks. Show a ghost preview of the rigid fit and allow depth and lateral adjustment for clearance. Confirm to attach the incoming part to its fixed parent.
- Temporarily hide obstructing parts or inspect the socket alone. Use move and rotate controls for manual placement when the connector lacks usable faces. Record the attachment and joint location for the PDF. Support undo and redo. Moving an attached parent carries its children while preserving their relative poses.
- Keep parts as independent meshes. Hide or isolate a piece for painting without changing its saved assembled pose. Treat each surface as a part ID and original face ID pair. The assembled pose and all attached parts drive light and shadows, even during isolation. Preserve committed paint and mask edits when a joint moves, and flag the generated light masks for review.

## Painting workflow

- Import multiple PNG, JPEG, or WebP reference images alongside the model or kit. Switch between references without losing the active recipe or its swatches. Use the eyedropper to fill tone slots in the same recipe from different images. Generate a shadow, midtone, and highlight suggestion from a selected rectangle or the whole active image. Let the painter review and edit the hex swatches and add paint names, brands, and mix notes.
- Set the model's upright direction and front. Create named regions and link them to reusable color recipes. Brush or erase shadow coverage on the visible surface. Preview a connected bucket fill from a clicked face, adjust crease sensitivity, and confirm the result. Respect disconnected pieces, OBJ groups, and other region assignments. Support undo and redo.
- Rotate a directional light by azimuth and elevation. Evaluate exposure from surface angle and self-shadowing. Preview two ordered cutoffs, initially 35% and 75%. Commit and edit the cumulative masks. Adding highlights adds midtone coverage there, and erasing midtones clears highlights there.
- Add an optional edge-highlight pass with its own recipe swatch. Preview raised ridges and bevels in the selected region, including on the shadowed side. Adjust sensitivity and surface band width, then brush in or erase stretches. Exclude recessed seams, unrelated region boundaries, and flat triangulation from suggestions. Keep committed coverage during preview and confirm before replacing manual edits.
- Create, name, and duplicate lighting snapshots. Each stores its own light, cutoffs, committed masks, and edge-highlight coverage. All snapshots share the palette, regions, and shadow coat. Confirm regeneration over brush edits and support undo. Regenerating midtones or highlights preserves the edge pass.
- Export the active snapshot's committed masks to Letter or A4 PDF. Put each named region in a part-by-pass table with swatches, hex values, and paint or mix notes. Let painters place optional numbered callouts in the model views and link them to the table. Use consistent front, back, left, and right orthographic views for each pass. Include an edge-highlight column and matching four-view page when the active snapshot has edge coverage. Flag missing swatches or unapplied light changes before export.
- Add numbered installation steps for multipart builds. Start with the virtual attachment order and let the painter reorder the physical steps, including assembling a group of parts before attaching it to the anchor. Show the incoming part, its mating part, the joint location, and optional notes in each step. Keep the assembled paint guide and let the painter include isolated paint views for selected parts.

## Geometry, rendering, and acceleration

- Use Electron, React, and Three.js for the desktop app and ship an x64 installer with Electron Forge. Bundle an x64 C++ geometry worker and expose bounded queries, progress, and cancellation to the renderer. Keep file access behind a narrow preload interface.
- Stream binary STL records with 64-bit offsets. Retain stable original face IDs within each part and spatially partition meshes into disk-backed batches. Match shared edges across partitions of the same part. Keep region and mask edits keyed by part and face ID so snapshots share geometry. Accept ASCII STL and geometry-only OBJ as well. The 2 GiB combined size target applies to binary STL.
- Use original-mesh adjacency for edge suggestions, including across internal partitions. Keep the band clipped to its named region and render the same committed surface coverage in the viewport and PDF.
- Use the GPU to draw the viewport, paint overlays, and offscreen PDF views. Use a lighter meshoptimizer preview while moving the camera and restore full-detail batches when it settles. Brush, bucket, mask generation, and PDF rendering use the original triangles.
- Implement full-resolution lighting in the native worker with Embree as the CPU reference and fallback. Evaluate surface angle and occlusion across partitions and cache exposure by mesh and light direction. Threshold-only changes reuse the cached scores.
- Evaluate D3D12 DirectX Raytracing for GPU occlusion on supported adapters. Test ray-tracing support and the available video memory budget before building acceleration structures. Commit results only when a full run completes for the current light revision. Use the same input geometry, exposure rules, and face IDs on both backends. Fall back to Embree if support or memory is insufficient, the GPU job fails, or the comparison fails validation. Keep GPU mask generation conditional on measured end-to-end gains.

## Portable projects

Save one versioned ZIP64 .paintproject archive containing all original model and kit files, each part's identity and staging status, the anchor, joint transforms, installation order and notes, and selected isolated print views. Also store reference images in display order, the active reference, orientation, palette, regions, snapshots with edge coverage, and PDF callout placements. Keep derived geometry in a rebuildable local cache. Stream the archive to a temporary sibling file and replace the destination after a successful write. Preserve the current project if another import or save fails.

## Validation

- Start with large-mesh import and picking on the supplied STL. Verify all 8,766,580 faces remain addressable, brush and bucket work after camera movement, and fills and shadows cross partition boundaries. Verify edited masks appear in the PDF.
- Use the supplied segmented kit to test staged alternatives, preserved authored placement, nearby snap after moving a part, six-face joins and manual placement, isolation, and a cross-part shadow. Verify weak or ambiguous snaps leave parts in place. Move a painted assembly joint, verify that local paint edits survive, and check that the light masks are flagged for review. Reopen the portable project and verify the transforms, installation order, and selected isolated views in the PDF.
- Exercise a generated binary STL and a generated multipart set just under 2 GiB each on 32 GB Windows hardware. Measure import time, UI responsiveness, peak process memory and disk use, mask generation, and PDF export. Verify cancellation cleans temporary data and the app stays responsive.
- For GPU lighting, compare committed masks on overhang and partition-boundary fixtures, the supplied STL, and the near-2 GiB fixture. Time initial setup and repeat light changes, including upload and readback. Record peak video memory use and adapter details. Verify CPU fallback for unsupported devices and insufficient GPU memory. The reference PC has an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 with DirectX Raytracing 1.1 support or newer, verified using the installed driver. Full-model benchmark timings are pending.
- Test the packaged app offline with a recipe sampled from two different images. Reopen a moved project after clearing its cache and moving the original inputs out of reach. Verify both images and the sampled swatches are restored and inspect both PDF paper sizes. Deliver an unsigned local installer unless signing credentials are available.
- Verify raised and recessed edges, narrow and wide bands, manual edits, and snapshot duplication. On the supplied STL, check that edge coverage survives project reopen and midtone or highlight regeneration. Print the guide and verify that the table, callouts, and edge band let a painter complete a region from the sheet.
